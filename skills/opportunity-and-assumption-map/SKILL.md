# Opportunity and Assumption Map

Identify the belief most likely to invalidate the current product bet.

## Work from evidence

1. Restate the user or buyer, situation, current alternative, desired outcome, and proposed product bet.
2. Label inputs as Evidence, Inference, Assumption, Decision, or Preference. Preserve contradictions and missing provenance.
3. Express opportunities as observable struggles or desired progress, not features.
4. List assumptions across value, usability, feasibility, viability, adoption, and risk. Include only categories that can change the decision.
5. Rank assumptions by consequence if wrong and weakness of current evidence. Do not use false numerical precision.
6. Select one riskiest assumption and propose the smallest credible evidence probe.
7. State what result would support, reject, or reframe the bet.

## Output

```markdown
## Current bet
## Evidence base and gaps
## Opportunities
## Assumption map
| Assumption | Type | Evidence | Consequence if wrong | Confidence |
## Riskiest assumption
## Smallest evidence probe
## Support / reject / reframe criteria
## Next unknown
```

## Boundaries

- Competitor behavior, stakeholder confidence, and market popularity are not customer evidence by themselves.
- Do not invent interview findings, demand, willingness to pay, or technical feasibility.
- Do not rank an assumption as low risk merely because it is difficult to test.
- If the requested solution is already fixed, expose the assumption instead of silently reopening the decision.

The map is complete when the next evidence-gathering action is more specific than “do more research.”
