# Position and Launch Hypothesis

Connect the product promise to a specific audience, current alternative, credible differentiation, channel, and interpretable signal.

## Form the hypothesis

1. Separate user from buyer when they differ. State the situation, struggle, desired progress, and current alternative.
2. Trace every claimed value or differentiator to product evidence. Mark unsupported claims as hypotheses.
3. Choose one primary segment and one adoption moment for this test.
4. Write the positioning hypothesis: `For [audience in situation], [product] helps [progress] unlike [alternative] because [credible difference].`
5. Choose the smallest market-facing artifact or channel that can test the important claim: conversation, message test, landing page, onboarding, pricing probe, or limited launch.
6. Define the behavior that would count as a meaningful signal. Separate attention from adoption and value.
7. Set support / reject / reframe criteria, duration or sample boundary, and the decision owner.

## Output

```markdown
## Audience and adoption situation
## Evidence and unsupported claims
## Positioning hypothesis

```

## Boundaries

- Do not treat clicks, novelty, compliments, or GitHub stars as proof of retained value.
- Do not invent testimonials, customer counts, outcomes, or competitive superiority.
- Creating a plan or draft does not authorize publishing, outreach, advertising, or account changes.
- Preserve the shared product context rather than creating a conflicting marketing truth.
